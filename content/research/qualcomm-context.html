3. What Hexagon-MLIR does

The paper describes an open-source compilation stack for the Hexagon NPU. It complements the commercial toolchains of Qualcomm. The authors call it a work in progress.

  • Inputs: PyTorch models through Torch-MLIR, and Triton kernels through triton-to-linalg.
  • Core dialects: linalg, tensor, scf, memref, arith, math, vector and async.
  • Passes: operator fusion, tiling with copies from DDR to TCM, HVX vectorization and multithreading.
  • More passes: double buffering with asynchronous DMA, and a path for matrix operations to a matrix engine.
  • Reported results: per-kernel speedups against a non-vectorized baseline. Examples: GELU in float16 at 63.9 times, RMS-norm in float16 at 46.5 times, flash attention in float32 at 4.7 times.
  • Limits: the paper has no end-to-end model results. It does not describe all passes. Some operations need control flow.
  • Hardware: Qualcomm says the stack works today on a single Hexagon NPU, tested on v73, v75 and v79.
