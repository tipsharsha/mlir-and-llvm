This chapter gives an outline for a first project. The project teaches the pipeline of Hexagon-MLIR with upstream MLIR, and it does not need an NPU. It matches directions RD-2 and RD-3 in the previous chapter.

NOTE:This is an outline, not a procedure. The pass names and options of MLIR change between versions. Weeks 4 to 6 give the exact commands. Do those weeks first.

1. Goal

Build a small fused, tiled and vectorized kernel on a CPU. Use a small fixed-size buffer in place of the TCM. Then compare the measured speedup with a model.

2. Prerequisites

  • Week 1: LLVM IR, opt and the vectorizer remarks.
  • Weeks 4 to 6: an MLIR build, linalg, tiling, bufferization and lowering to LLVM.
  • The Hexagon-MLIR paper (R16). Read its sections on fusion, tiling, vectorization and double buffering.

3. Milestones

  1. Write GELU or RMS-norm as linalg operations on tensors.
  2. Fuse the producer and consumer generics. Check that the intermediate tensor is gone.
  3. Tile the fused operation. Choose tile sizes so that the working set fits the buffer that stands in for the TCM.
  4. Vectorize the tile with the vector dialect. Use a vector width of 128 bytes, the width of HVX in the 2025 slides.
  5. Add double buffering by hand: a ping-pong pair of buffers, with a prologue and a loop body.
  6. Measure the run time for each step above. Use the same data and the same machine.
  7. Compare the result with the model 1/max(m, 1−m) of the paper. Find the ratio m of your kernel.
  8. Write one page: what the model predicted, what you measured, and the reason for the difference.

4. What you learn

  • How fusion, tiling, vectorization and buffering fit together in one MLIR pipeline.
  • Why a CPU with caches does not show the same effects as a scratchpad. This is the first limit of the project, and it is a result.
  • How hard it is to choose tile sizes by hand. This leads to RD-2.

5. Next step at work

These questions help you to connect the project to the real stack. Ask them in your team.

  • Which team owns Hexagon-MLIR, and which team owns the Mojo backend for Hexagon?
  • Do the two share kernels, passes or tests?
  • Can you get access to a Hexagon device or the SDK and the simulator?
  • Which parts can you publish, and who approves?
